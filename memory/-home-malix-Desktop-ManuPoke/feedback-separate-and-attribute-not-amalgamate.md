When a finding claims two consequences (e.g. "~50% theft of a SOLVENT borrower **and** LP bad debt"), the user demands they be proven to be the SAME event on the SAME state, or presented as two DISTINCT points and labelled as such. Silent amalgamation ("theft plus bad debt") reads to a triager as one event and collapses trust on the whole report when they notice the bad-debt PoC ran at a different (deeper/insolvent-edge) state than the solvent headline.

Two hard rules that came out of the Ammalgam saturation-premium finding:
1. **Separate the states — but sweep the parameter space before concluding a negative.** I first concluded the solvent-point seize does NOT create bad debt (tested ONE point: 100:1, borrow 10.2 → 50% seize, leverage reverts `LiquidationZeroPremium`) and reported "two separate points, not one event." That was an un-executed hypothesis dressed as a conclusion (First Maxim). Sweeping (ratio, borrow) later found the single-event scope-killer DID exist: at 50:1 with borrow ≈sqrt(X*Y) (a borrow right at the registration threshold → tiny `oldSat` → premium blows up), a SOLVENT position (control opens) is seized ~98% AND turned into ~8 L of LP bad debt in one event, baseline 0 under identical funding. Lesson: a per-position property (seize magnitude, bad-debt) can depend hard on a second parameter (here borrow-vs-registration-threshold); "X doesn't happen" from one point is not a result. Exactly 100% seize makes the realization path panic (division by a zeroed deposit), so the clean bad-debt figure is the ~98% point, not 100%.
2. **Attribute on ALL paths.** Before crediting your vector with a loss, show every OTHER external path realises zero of it on the same state. Here: on the unseized position, leverage reverts `LiquidationZeroPremium` AND HARD (even fully funded) reverts `LiquidationPremiumTooHigh` — both burn 0 bad debt. Only then is the 0-vs-11 L delta attributable to the seize alone. A "reverts X" is only clean if X is a health/ceiling revert, not a funding revert (`NotEnoughRepaidForLiquidation` means the path WOULD proceed if funded — fund it fully and re-check).

Also: "generalizes across shapes" is false if the two shapes are the same RATIO at different scale (100:1 vs 500:5). Test genuinely different ratios (100:1 → 49.7%, 1000:1 → 16.4%) and state magnitude as shape-dependent. And distinguish a solvency reject (`AmmalgamLTV`/leverage) from a mere borrow-cap (`AmmalgamMaxBorrowReached`) before calling a point "insolvent" — a failed control-open is not automatically insolvency.

When your OWN honesty narrows the finding (it only fires on a rare position shape, e.g. a ≥9:1 asymmetric straddle while balanced ones are safe), that reopens the likelihood/"expected on-chain" eligibility gate — a real mechanism on a position nobody opens gets classed low or out-of-scope. Don't leave it to the triager: (1) MAP the threshold empirically (sweep the parameter, isolate the driver — here, hold sqrt(X*Y) and leverage constant to separate "asymmetry" from "leverage"; find the exact frontier and confirm the central/textbook case is safe), (2) anchor the threshold in the mechanism from source (what gates registration), and (3) POSE the severity verdict yourself, arguing against your own higher tier where honest (I argued High-not-Critical because the balanced case is provably safe). State the precondition CONJUNCTION (shape AND utilization AND drift AND passivity) as the reachability bound, since the intersection, not any single term, sizes the victim population.

When a worst-case magnitude depends on a parameter, measure the WIDTH of the band that produces it before leading the headline with it. My ~98% seize needed the borrow within ~2% of the registration threshold (sweep: borrow 10.0→98%, 10.2→48%, 12.0→15%); the robust case was ~11-48% and the LP bad debt only existed at the ~98% knife-edge. Leading on 98% reads as an unrepresentative extreme to any triager who sweeps the parameter. Lead on the robust range, cite the extreme as the maximum with its band width stated. This is the mirror of "don't under-sell": don't headline a razor's-edge either. Also: rest a loss on the structural inevitability (a stripped borrower won't repay 13 L to recover 1 X, so the borrow goes bad and is realized when the husk is liquidated), NOT on an actor's incentive to act (the attacker gains nothing from closing the husk — a triager asks "why would they").

**Why:** over-claiming a bundled/attributed loss is the same error as conceding a gate you didn't exhaust — the verdict must be exactly what the executed artifacts support, split by state. **How to apply:** for every multi-effect finding, run a per-state, per-path matrix (which liquidation/exit paths fire, with what selector, at what drift) and write the report around that matrix, not around the headline. Prefer the architectural invariant (source-level: who WRITES the per-account baseline) over a single PoC run that might have missed the triggering condition.
