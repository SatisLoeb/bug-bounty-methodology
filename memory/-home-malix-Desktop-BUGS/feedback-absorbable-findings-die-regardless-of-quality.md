**Operator's rule, 2026-08-08, after 3F grunt closed duplicate:** when a program has published an
exclusion in your finding's category, the finding is probably unpayable no matter how good your
distinction is, **because the party who decides whether the distinction holds is the party who pays.**
Interpreting your own clause broadly costs them nothing.

**Generalised by the record.** OUTCOMES.jsonl at 87 rows: **one payment, $10,000, Polymarket,
access-control, WEB surface.** Every submitted-and-closed-at-$0 row died the same way, via four
interchangeable mechanisms: prior report (6 duplicates), known class (3 informatives, "class already
known to triage"), published clause (3F), declared-trusted actor (Ammalgam, Perena, Doppler). The
mechanism was CONCEDED every time. Cantina's reviewer even validated the methodology on 3F, which
almost never happens. **Nothing technical was refuted in any of them.**

So the property to test is not "is there an exclusion" but **ABSORBABLE**: is there any pre-existing
artifact — clause, prior report, known class, trusted-role framing — that lets the payer file this
finding without ever engaging the mechanism? If yes, correctness is irrelevant.

**My specific failure, worth naming because it will recur.** I produced the right diagnosis on 3F
("you made that reading expensive to hold, not impossible") and drew the wrong conclusion from it:
harden the argument. The operator asked me to strengthen the paragraph rebutting "economically
imperfect", I strengthened it, and I never said that **the need to strengthen it WAS the answer.**
Three paragraphs of scope rebuttal is a measurement of absorbability, not a defence against it.

**The hardest part:** [[feedback-target-diet-is-the-binding-constraint]] already said the money is
won or lost at sourcing, and it was written BEFORE this engagement. It did not bind. A lesson that
does not change the day-0 decision is an archive, not a lesson.

**How to apply. A deletion, not a process.** A category exclusion is a Phase 0 abandon, not a section
to write. If the distinction does not fit in ONE sentence without arguing about the bullet's wording,
do not take the target. Do not write the rebuttal section at all — writing it is the tell.

**Where the rule bends, and it is a sourcing criterion not a pleading one:** it rests on *the payer
decides*. In a CONTEST (C4, Sherlock, audit competition) a judge rules against a published rulebook
and the sponsor cannot absorb unilaterally. Same finding, same clause, different expected value,
because the free option sits with someone else. At equal technical quality, prefer targets where the
decider is not the payer. See [[feedback-oos-bullet-describing-your-finding-is-its-tombstone]] for the
3F instance and [[ev-gate-check-program-responsiveness-not-just-severity]].
